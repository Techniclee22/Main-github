# TTRPG Character Portrait Personality & Anti-AI Framework

Grounded in the visual identity of *Daggerheart* (Darrington Press) and modern emotive tabletop art (*Humblewood*, *D&D 5e*, *Pathfinder 2e*). This framework turns static fantasy archetypes into unforgettable, soulful characters that players immediately want to inhabit, while actively counteracting the synthetic "AI look."

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy: "Character First, Weapon Second"

Classic tabletop art often asks: *"What does this character do in combat?"* (brandished weapons, aggressive stances, tactical gear). Emotive tabletop art asks: *"Who is this person, what have they survived, and what do they love?"*

When designing hero portraits, prioritize emotional accessibility, warmth, and narrative history over cold combat utility.

---

## 2. The 7 Pillars of Maximum Personality

### 1. Asymmetry of History (Lived-In Imperfection)
Symmetrical, pristine figures look like 3D mannequins or generic stock assets. Personality lives in physical history:
- **Scars & Quirks**: A notched ear, a chipped horn, a cracked lens, an old sting mark from a bumblebee, or an off-kilter mechanical ear.
- **Mending with Care**: Visible mismatched stitching, a patched velvet hem, brass wire bracing on a cracked wooden handle, or solder marks on copper chassis plates.
- **Asymmetric Expression**: A slight tilt in a smile, one eyebrow raised higher in curiosity, or an inquisitive 10–15° head cock.

### 2. The Window to the Soul (Eye Geometry & Specular Drama)
Eyes carry 80% of perceived empathy and emotion:
- **Pupil & Iris Design**: Avoid cold, empty black holes or aggressive slitted slits unless mechanically intended. Use warm amber, copper, or moss tones with organic slit shapes (cross-stars, horizontal bars, glowing lens apertures).
- **Multi-Point Moisture Highlights**: A crisp primary highlight catching ambient skylight, plus a soft secondary warm bounce reflection from clothing, magic, or the ground.
- **Micro-Angles**: Even for non-organic beings (like Clanks or Warforged), convey soul through adjustable shutter brows, warm glowing vacuum-tube filaments, or curved aperture lids.

### 3. The Rule of Three Personal Narrative Trinkets
Every memorable character carries at least three personal storytelling items visibly integrated into their attire:
1. **A Memory**: A braided wildflower bracelet, an enamel pin from an old companion, a lucky copper gear, or an origami crane tucked into a belt.
2. **A Tool of Wonder**: A glowing corked vial of bioluminescent swamp moss, a tiny glass herbarium terrarium, or an enchanted brass pendulum.
3. **A Relic of the Road**: An antique scratched brass compass, a bundle of drying chamomile tied with twine, or a notched journal hanging from a leather thong.

### 4. Familiar or Companion Bond
Nothing establishes immediate warmth faster than how a small living creature interacts with the character.
- A glowing iridescent dragonfly resting cheek-to-cheek on a ranger's shoulder.
- A one-eyed barn owl nesting atop a mechanical witch's hat.
- A plump bumblebee drinking sweet honey water from a robot's brass finger.
The companion acts as an external seal of trustworthiness and gentleness.

### 5. Tactile Material Contrast (The "Huggable" Texture Hierarchy)
Depth and charm come from juxtaposing contrasting physical textures:
- **Organic vs. Manufactured**: Weathered brass and polished walnut wood paired with knitted wool, coarse burlap, velvet moss, or living vine tendrils.
- **Weathered vs. Luminous**: Patinated copper verdigris accented by the radiant glow of an internal arcane furnace or enchanted phials.
- **Visible Craft**: Knife carving facets on wood, hand-woven cordage, visible watchmaker screws, and soft embroidered floral borders.

### 6. Postural Curiosity & Candid Energy
Avoid static military mugshots or sterile T-poses:
- **Head Tilt**: A 10–15° inquisitive cock of the head signals active listening, wonder, and empathy.
- **Candid Gesture**: Touching a favorite trinket, carefully balancing a delicate tea cup, gently cradling a botanical specimen, or adjusting a crooked hat.
- **Open Silhouette**: A relaxed, inviting stance rather than a defensive, closed-off guard.

### 7. Warm Atmospheric Lighting & Environmental Framing
Lighting establishes emotional safety and wonder:
- **Golden-Hour Sunbeams / Hearthlight**: Warm, low-angle light that casts rich amber highlights.
- **Subsurface Scattering**: Light glowing through translucent materials (leaf cloaks, frog throat pouches, amber gemstones, glass apothecary tubes).
- **Magical Particulates**: Soft atmospheric motes, floating golden pollen, drift embers, or tiny glowing fireflies that provide a dreamy, storybook bokeh.

## 3. The Anti-AI Diagnostic & De-Glazing Rubric

To strip away the synthetic "AI look" and make a piece feel authentically painted by human concept artists, systematically test and eliminate these 6 universal AI tells:

### Tell 1: Universal Specular Wax Sheen
- **The AI Tell**: Every surface (skin, cloth, iron, hair, stone) has an identical, oily or airbrushed plastic reflection.
- **The Human Fix**: Enforce strict Bidirectional Reflectance (BRDF). Matte wool and woven cloth must be completely non-reflective, flat, and light-absorbing. Weathered leather has dull, scuffed specular hits. Only wet eyes, polished glass, and burnished metal receive pinpoint highlights.

### Tell 2: Escher Overlaps & Melted Tangencies
- **The AI Tell**: Straps merge into cloth; hair braids dissolve into cloaks; strings pass through arms without anchoring; lines blur where objects meet.
- **The Human Fix**: Structural clarity. Every strap has a buckle, ring, or rivet. Tailoring seams follow human garment construction. Accessories have physical depth and cast definitive occlusion shadows onto the layer beneath them.

### Tell 3: High-Entropy Greeble & Senseless Filigree
- **The AI Tell**: Infinite tiny decorative squiggles, fake runes, pseudo-lace, and random clockwork gears that serve no mechanical or aesthetic purpose.
- **The Human Fix**: Intentional economy of mark-making. Limit costume details to large, readable shapes. Detail only the focal points (face, eyes, hands). Leave large, resting planar areas on clothing and backgrounds to give the viewer's eye relief.

### Tell 4: The "Floating Embers & Volumetric Glaze" Cliché
- **The AI Tell**: Inescapable golden floating dust motes, firefly sparkles, teal-and-magenta rim lights, and vaseline-blurred edges filling every quadrant.
- **The Human Fix**: Natural directional lighting with clean, graphic values. Use single dominant key lighting with soft local fill. Establish clear negative space. Replace floating generic sparkles with grounded physical environmental storytelling (e.g. realistic drying herbs, wood grain, or honest parchment texture).

### Tell 5: Pupil & Catchlight Inconsistency
- **The AI Tell**: Misshapen pupil boundaries, fractured iris geometry, or specular catchlights that appear on opposite sides of each eye (implying contradictory suns).
- **The Human Fix**: Anatomically and geometrically coherent gaze. Bilateral light consistency—both eyes must catch the primary light source from the exact same directional vector.

### Tell 6: Soft Pillowy Edges (Lost Edge Control)
- **The AI Tell**: Over-blended, airbrushed outlines with an unnatural cut-out perimeter glow that makes characters feel pasted on.
- **The Human Fix**: Masterful "Lost and Found" edges. Hard, confident strokes on silhouettes and focal planes; soft, lost edges where forms blend into deep shadow or atmospheric background. Visible, intentional gouache or oil brushwork that shows the hand of the artist.

## 4. The Non-Realism Rule: Escaping the Hyper-Rendered 3D Trap

When users say an image looks "too realistic," they are reacting to diffusion models defaulting to simulated 3D CGI volume, photographic depth of field, micro-surface pilling/grain, and raytraced lighting calculations. Even when instructed to use traditional media, models frequently generate what looks like a photographed resin miniature or a hyper-rendered 3D video game asset.

### The Antidote: True 2D Storybook & Comic Stylization
To make a character feel genuinely drawn by human illustrators and irresistible to tabletop players:
1. **Bold 2D Graphic Inking**: Visible, imperfect pen-and-ink lines, hand crosshatching, scratchy dip-pen contours, and deliberate line weight variations (like Quentin Blake, Tony DiTerlizzi, or Mike Mignola).
2. **Flat / Translucent Washes**: Pure watercolor stains or clean two-tone cel shading. Reject simulated micro-textures (no rendered yarn loops, no photographic rust grain).
3. **Pushed, Whimsical Silhouette**: Exaggerated, cartoony shape language (a teapot-round boiler, stubby legs, comically oversized floppy hats, kettle-spout arms that puff steam when happy).
4. **Organic Sketchbook Imperfection**: Uncalculated asymmetric poses, casual handwritten marginalia, ink splatters, and loose gestures that feel pulled straight from an artist's personal adventure journal.

---

## 5. The Graphic Economy Rule (Production Art vs. AI Over-Detailing)

A massive tell of AI art is **hyper-fiddly detailing**: microscopic scratches, infinite filigree, dense skin wrinkles, and endless tiny rivets across every square inch. Real human production concept art (animation pitch art, comic books, tabletop rulebook spots) relies on **Graphic Economy**:
1. **Simplification & Large Shape Reads**: It takes immense human labor to draw every individual stitch or wrinkle. Real production artists simplify forms into clean, bold shapes that read instantly from across the room.
2. **Zero Fiddly Greeble**: Drop the tiny micro-rivets, unnecessary seamlines, and obsessive surface pilling. Replace them with broad, confident color planes and clean graphic silhouettes.
3. **Age & Facial Cleanliness**: AI defaults to rendering heavily weathered, haggard, overly lined skin. For a younger, vigorous look, keep the facial planes smooth and clear, defining character through silhouette, expression, and eye intensity rather than wrinkles.
4. **Clean Weapon & Prop Icons**: A weapon should look like a bold, readable prop design for an animated series or comic book—iconic silhouette, clear functional masses, not a mess of 50 tiny disconnected gears.

## 6. Class & Ancestry Adaptation Guide

### For Clanks / Warforged (Mechanical Beings):
- **Avoid Cold Terminology**: Replace "chrome, cybernetic, robot, killer drone" with "antique brass, polished walnut, clockwork hearth, copper tea kettle, ticking music-box soul".
- **Expressive Mechanical Features**: Give them animated shutter brows, soft glowing filament eyes, porcelain faceplates, or a mini chimney releasing pleasant lavender-scented steam.
- **The "Gentle Giant" Contrast**: A sturdy clockwork automaton doing something delicate (brewing floral tea, pressing wild violets into an herbarium, tending a sleeping kitten).

### For Small / Amphibious Folk (Ribbets, Halflings, Goblins):
- **Proportions**: Slightly chubby, soft silhouettes with oversized, dewy expressive eyes and expressive cheek pouches.
- **Nature Integration**: Cloaks crafted from living leaves, acorn caps used as toggles, reed quivers, and lily-pad canopies.

### For Feline Folk (Katari / Tabaxi):
- **Dynamic Ear & Whiskers Language**: Expressive feline ears that swivel or tilt to signal mood (curiosity, alertness, playful mischief) and expressive whisker twitches.
- **Warm, Feline Eye Geometry**: Large, luminous almond-to-round eyes with vertical-slit-to-wide pupils and deep emotional warmth.
- **Feline Fluidity in Armor**: Supple, tailored leathers and woven textiles with lightweight brass/bronze fastenings rather than clunky, rigid plate that hinders acrobatic agility.
- **Playful Cat Quirks**: A favorite bell, braided mane beads, a tucked feather toy, or a habit of grooming a paw mid-sentence.

## 7. Strict Modification Protocol: Preventing Unrequested Feature Drift

When modifying an existing character based on user feedback, generative pipelines often commit two major errors:
1. **Unrequested Feature Drift / Mutation**: While altering one feature (e.g. face shape), the model re-rolls other established features (e.g. eye color, weapon designs, background elements).
2. **Hallucinated Creative Additions**: Injecting unprompted details (e.g. turning "hag's eyes" into split green/purple eyes with runes when not requested).

### The 4 Rules for Iteration Integrity:
1. **Explicit Constraint Locking**: Isolate what is CHANGING vs. what is LOCKED. Use a strict enumerated list in the generation prompt:
   - `LOCKED (DO NOT ALTER)`: Exact weapons, eye colors, wardrobe, setting.
   - `TARGETED CHANGE`: The single specific modification requested by the user.
2. **Negative Disqualification**: Explicitly forbid the drift (e.g. "Both eyes must be identical glowing yellow. Do not make eyes green or purple. Do not change the weapons.").
3. **Inspect Before Delivery**: Run the verification tool immediately against the user's literal prompt constraints. If a locked element drifted, discard and re-roll before presenting.
4. **Zero Unprompted Embellishments**: Do not invent lore or visual gimmicks unless explicitly asked. Stick strictly to user specifications.

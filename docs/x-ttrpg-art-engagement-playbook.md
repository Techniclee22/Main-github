## Part 1 — What Gets Traction on X

Research scope: art, AI art, RPG/TTRPG, Daggerheart, and D&D accounts. Roughly one week of activity sampled via search + account deep-dives.

### Volume (7-day window)

| Topic | Approx. posts (7d) |
| --- | ---: |
| AI art | ~151,000 |
| Fantasy/digital art with images | ~49,000 |
| RPG/TTRPG art & characters | ~1,600 |
| D&D + art | ~700 |
| Daggerheart | ~130 |

Daggerheart is a **small, hungry niche**. Absolute numbers are lower; engagement rate on good posts can still be excellent.

---

### Pattern 1 — Finished character art, almost no caption

Strongest pattern across D&D / fantasy / Daggerheart:

- One strong full-body or portrait
- Caption like: `Commission character #oc #dnd`
- Clear silhouette (armor, helmet, unusual heritage)

**@EndariArt** (~737 followers) is the clearest overperformer:

| Post type | Likes | Bookmarks | Impressions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Helmet commission `#oc #dnd` | ~2,955 | ~622 | ~30k |
| Another `#oc #dnd` commission | ~1,692 | ~326 | ~15k |
| Strong fantasy/game commission | ~1,767 | ~272 | ~13k |
| Weaker / generic commissions | 13–28 | ~5 | &lt;1k |

Same account, same format — **design quality** decides whether a post does 15 likes or 3,000.

**Replies on strong commission posts:** “cool design,” helmet vs hair preference, commission asks / DMs.

---

### Pattern 2 — Known-character memes / format remixes

**@Dove_and_Crow** (~19k): Tiamat in a viral meme format → ~9.5k likes, ~1.4k bookmarks, ~30 replies.

Reply themes:

- Lore jokes (“she’s screaming on the inside”)
- Head recognition / humor
- Light table banter (“table is about to be incinerated”)

Memes travel farther than pretty art alone when they hitch a ride on an existing format.

---

### Pattern 3 — Reference sheets & “useful art”

- Character sheets / ref sheets get **saved** (high bookmarks)
- Example: sheet-style posts hitting ~1,700 likes on mid-size accounts
- Small accounts (~400 followers) still getting 150+ likes on ref sheets

People treat these as **tools**, not just aesthetics — perfect for GM/player aids.

---

### Pattern 4 — Interactive bait (replies over vanity likes)

- “Reply with your PNG, I’ll assign a magic item” → high reply count even when likes are modest
- Art raffles (follow / like / RT / drop OC) → reply + RT heavy

Good for **growth**; weaker for pure like counts.

## Daggerheart Specifically

~130 posts/week — niche volume is tiny vs AI art or general fantasy art.

### What works

- Finished **PC commissions** named by heritage/class flavor (faun bard, Infernis, Ribbet)
- Small artists punching hard: e.g. ~1.1k followers getting **170–230 likes** on DH commissions (~15–20% of follower base)
- Pro illustrators: Leesha Hannigan (~45k) Ribbet for Daggerheart → **~534 likes** (stronger than many of her other recent posts)
- Official FOMO/product posts from Darrington Press do okay
- Community chatter wants: Foundry tools, dice, separate GM screen

### What underperforms

- Go-live spam
- Vague “I want to play Daggerheart”
- Hashtag salad with no art
- Process confession / “first attempt / not going well” framing

## AI Art: Two Different Games

| Type | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Fan-service AI of **known characters** (franchise faces, anime) | Low-follower accounts can hit 1k–4k likes |
| Anti-AI callouts / “support real artists” | Huge engagement via drama |
| Plain “AI art” with no IP hook | Noise; weaker in TTRPG circles |

TTRPG art communities still reward **hand-drawn commission culture**. Heavy AI-art aesthetic association is risky if you want trust with players/GMs who buy aids.

**Practical note for Grok-assisted work:** lead with *character + tool*, not with “AI art” branding. Avoid QT’ing generic AI-art share threads (wrong audience).

## Part 2 — Playbook for @technicleecreat

### Current snapshot (as of research)

- ~19 followers, brand-new account
- Already shipping: character portraits, Veil foldable sheet, adversary cards, mechanic blogs
- Site content is strong (Fear, Experiences, campaign frames, environments, one-shots)
- Gap: posts often read like studio notes instead of **shareable tools + characters**

### Positioning (one line)

**Daggerheart table kit:** character art that wants a sheet, and sheets that want a character.

Everything on X should look like either a **PC** or a **tool in use**. Soft landscapes and “portrait experiments aren’t going well” don’t pull this crowd.

## Content Engine for Grok

**Pair every tool with a named character.**

Example: Veil isn’t “a sheet experiment.”  
Veil is *Veil, Infernis Wizard — foldable DH sheet (front = creation, back = SRD mechanics).*

### Three post types (80% of output)

| Type | Visual | Caption shape | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Character drop** | Full-body or bust, clear heritage/class silhouette | Name + class/heritage + 1 vibe line + `#Daggerheart` | Likes / follows |
| **Tool drop** | Sheet/card filled for that character (not blank PDF) | What it does in one line + free site or Gumroad CTA | Bookmarks / clicks |
| **Hook** | Same art + prompt | “Reply with your class + heritage — I’ll mock a sheet layout” | Replies / growth |

Skip process confession posts. If Grok makes a dud portrait, don’t post it.

---

## Weekly Cadence (~5 posts)

1. **Mon — Character** (hero image, short caption)
2. **Tue — Tool** (that character’s sheet / card / tracker)
3. **Wed — Hook** (reply bait)
4. **Thu — Blog bait** (one mechanic tip + link to site)
5. **Fri — Pack tease** (adversary set / sheet pack → Gumroad when ready)

One soft personal post max per week, and only if it’s still a strong image.

## What to Stop

- QT’ing generic AI-art share threads (wrong audience)
- Long “first attempt / playtest soon / not going well” framing
- Orphan landscapes with no table use
- Explaining the whole sheet in the caption — let the image + one line carry it
- Multiple competing CTAs in one post
